    What I have been thinking about

    I am Michał — a hands-on data science manager and AI systems builder. For the last decade and a half I have moved from research, through web products, into data science and the leadership of a small team. The constant underneath all of it is the same job: I help people make progress when technical and organizational systems become too complex to hold in their heads. I am the architect and the reviewer; increasingly, AI is the executor. I still prefer to keep my hands in the work.

    My strongest pillar is experimentation integrity — not the heroics of clever tests, but the unglamorous discipline of surfacing wrongness early enough that nobody ships it. I once watched a confident result reported at p=5e-6 collapse into a clean null at p=0.59 once we audited the raw counts. Trust, it turns out, is not a feeling. It is an error-surfacing system you build on purpose.

    What I have been thinking about lately

    Three things keep circling back. First, that the interesting unit is not the prompt — it is the workspace: durable, agent-readable context that lowers cognitive load and lets people work more independently inside a complicated stack. Second, that good experimentation is less about being right and more about making errors visible before they propagate. And third — quietly, almost sentimentally — the return of the homepage: a place you own, written in the open, that is not a feed and not a résumé. The same build pattern keeps showing up whether I am diagnosing a recommendation system at work, writing a hard-SF novel with adversarial critique rounds, or running a homework pipeline for my kids. The repetition is the signal.
